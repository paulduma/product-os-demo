# The Product OS Roadmap — From "curious" to "runs on agents"

*A step-by-step path for product managers who want to actually use AI in their daily work, not just read about it.*

## Why this exists

Most "how to use AI as a PM" content is a wall of tips. Nobody works through a wall of tips. This roadmap is built the other way: a small number of levels, each one forcing you to *do* something real before you move on. No level requires believing the next one yet — you just need to finish the one in front of you.

There's no scoring system, no badges to unlock. The gamification is the narrative itself: each level has a name, a clear finish line, and a visible jump in stakes from the one before it. You know where you are because what you're doing has gotten more real.

Everything referenced below (skills, the demo spec/benchmark/ticket examples) lives in the Product OS demo — this roadmap is the guided tour through it.

---

## Level 1 — Explorer
**Goal: pick a skill, make it yours.**

Go to the skill library, choose *any* skill — it doesn't matter which one, pick whatever sounds closest to something you actually do (writing a spec, running a competitive benchmark). Run it in Claude Code or Cowork on a throwaway topic. No stakes, no real project yet.

Then personalize it: open the skill, tweak the wording, adjust the output format to match how you actually write. This is the step most people skip, and it's the one that turns "a tool someone gave me" into "my tool."

**What "good" looks like:** the [Specs (fake)](notion) and [Benchmarks (fake)](notion) pages in the demo are exactly the shape a skill produces — read them before you run anything, so you know what you're aiming for.

**You're done with this level when:** you've run a skill twice — once as-is, once after editing it — and the second output actually sounds like you.

---

## Level 2 — Practitioner
**Goal: bring it into your real work.**

Same skill (or a new one), but this time point it at your own codebase — personal project or something at work. This is the first level with real stakes: real code, real constraints, no more sandbox.

This is also where the shape of the demo starts to matter for real: a spec you write here should look like something that could become the [tickets](linear) in the demo — same structure, same level of detail, just about something you actually care about shipping.

**You're done with this level when:** a skill has produced something — a spec, a benchmark — about a real problem you have, and you'd be willing to show it to a colleague.

---

## Level 3 — Shipper
**Goal: close the loop, alone, end to end.**

Take a spec's output and turn it into a ticket yourself. Implement it. Open a pull request. Merge it to GitHub. This is the level where "using AI" stops being about reading good output and starts being about *shipping* — you're now responsible for something that exists in production, or at least in a real repo.

This is deliberately the hardest jump in the roadmap. Levels 1 and 2 are about trust in the tool; level 3 is about trust in yourself using it on something that counts.

**You're done with this level when:** you have a merged PR that started as a skill's output.

---

## Level 4 — Operator
**Goal: install your own OS.**

Stop treating this as a tool you occasionally reach for. Set up your own Product OS — skills, a `CLAUDE.md`, a project template — on your machine, and make it the way you start every project from now on. This is the level where it stops being a novelty and starts being infrastructure.

**You're done with this level when:** starting a new project without your OS feels like it's missing something.

---

## Level 5 — Director
**Goal: delegate, don't execute.**

The final shift: from running skills yourself to supervising agents that run multi-step work on your behalf. Your job becomes deciding and validating, not doing. This is the level almost nobody starts at — and that's the point of the roadmap: you don't get to skip to "I have agents working for me" without having done the work that makes you able to tell whether what they produce is any good.

**You're done with this level when:** you're spending more time reviewing agent output than producing your own.

---

## How to use this

Don't try to plan all five levels in advance. Finish the one you're on, then look at the next one. If a level doesn't apply to your situation yet, that's fine — the point isn't speed, it's that each level is genuinely more real than the last.
